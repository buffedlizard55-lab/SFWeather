Reality check — read this first

No source on Earth forecasts a specific day in January 2027 today.

The National Weather Service publishes a daily forecast that reaches …. Beyond that, the official products are probabilities for a period (6-10 days, 8-14 days, weeks 3-4, months, 3-month seasons) — never a value for one named day.

So this scoreboard shows, for each day from 1 Oct 2026 to 31 Jan 2027, one of two things — and it always says which:

  • NWS forecast the day is inside the official forecast horizon, and every number is the actual NWS gridded forecast.
  • Climatology the day is beyond it, so every number is the 1991-2020 observed record for that calendar date at the verified NOAA station. This is not a forecast — it is what has historically happened on that date.

Long rain-duration events (“days or weeks of straight rain”)

      

        
A wet day is a calendar day with at least 0.01 in of
          liquid precipitation. A streak is consecutive wet days inside
          1 Oct–31 Jan. Counted from the actual observed record, 1991-2020, station USW00023272. For landlord: a week-long wet spell is close to a coin flip in any given year (53.3% of seasons), and a 10-day spell happens ~1 year in 4.

Wind, gusts, and wind + rain together

NWS forecast verification — how good the 7-day forecast has actually been at 94122

      

        
Every night the pipeline saves the 7-day NWS forecast it just fetched; once a
          forecast day is far enough in the past to have an observation at the NOAA
          GHCN/GSOD station, it is scored against what actually fell. This card
          reports the running score. It starts empty because the current forecast
          window (last 8 days) has not yet observed — the first scored pairs appear
          as soon as the season gets under way. Source: NWS gridpoint forecasts
          archived in data/forecast_history.json; observations from
          GHCN-Daily USW00023272.

        

      

    


    
    

      
CPC back-test — how often a CPC above-median tilt actually verified here

      

        
A CPC "above-normal precipitation" probability for a 3-month season is only
          useful if, over the years, those tilts have actually correlated with wetter
          seasons at this point. Once enough archived CPC issuances have
          been sampled at the 94122 centroid, this card will show the observed
          hit-rate for "above-median" and "below-median" tilts for the OND / NDJ /
          DJF / JFM seasons that cover Oct–Jan. The back-test machinery is in place
          (shapefile sampling per issuance, season-total computation from GHCN-Daily);
          issuances from before today cannot be fetched from the live CPC GIS server
          (it only hosts the latest month), so a back-fill from CPC's static archive
          is required. Status below. Source: CPC seasonal shapefile archives
          seasprcp_YYYYMM.zip / seastemp_YYYYMM.zip at
          ftp.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/GIS/us_tempprcpfcst/.

        

      

    


    
    
    

      
Model guidance — NMME ensemble maps NOT AN OFFICIAL FORECAST

      

        
This tier exists because the raw multi-model ensemble is worth looking at —
          and because it must never be confused with a forecast. The National Weather Service
          forecast and CPC’s official seasonal outlook are the products the rest of this site
          is built from; the North American Multi-Model Ensemble behind them is experimental model
          output that no forecaster has signed off on for this ZIP code. Nothing in this section is
          converted into a probability, an amount or a wind speed, and nothing here is merged into
          the day-by-day scoreboard — a rule the verification ledger
          enforces (model-guidance-isolation), not a promise. The maps below are local
          copies of NOAA’s own images; the wording that explains what their contours mean is
          copied verbatim from NOAA’s description page and re-checked against the fetched text
          on every run. Sources:
          CPC NMME ·
          NMME probability forecasts ·
          how to read them ·
          real-time forecast archive.

Verification — every number checked against its source

      

        
The pipeline re-reads its own datasets every night and checks, one claim at
          a time, that the number shown is the number in the official file, that the file was
          actually retrieved (with the HTTP status, byte count and SHA-256 of those exact bytes
          recorded), and that the project’s rules were respected — no day labelled a forecast
          outside the official horizon, no humidity value presented as an observation when it is a
          derivation, no NOAA test message shown as a real warning. A failed check stops the build.

        

      

    


    
    

      
Two official answers — NOAA’s published normals vs this project’s own count

      

        
The single most useful cross-check on this site. NOAA publishes, for every
          calendar date at this station, its own probability that the date records at least
          0.01 / 0.25 / 1.00 in of rain. This project also counts that from 30 seasons of
          GHCN-Daily. Rather than pick one, both are published here with the difference.
          Source: NCEI 1991–2020 daily normals (USW00023272).

Known limitations & next work

      

        

        
Structural limits of this project

        
    
          
  1. No daily forecast exists beyond the NWS horizon. Every day outside it
            shows observed climatology, clearly badged. Anyone who tells you the rainfall on
            14 January 2027 is “going to be 0.8 inches” is inventing it. Verify: forecast.weather.gov MapClick only shows 7 days.
    2. 
          
  2. Two different stations. Rain/temperature from the San Francisco
            downtown gauge USW00023272, wind from SFO 72494023234. They are 11.9 miles apart, and San Francisco’s
            microclimates mean the Sunset differs from both. Sources: GHCN-Daily file and GSOD archive.
    3. 
          
  3. GSOD days are UTC days (00-24Z), i.e. roughly 16:00-16:00
            local, while GHCN rain days are local days. The joint wind-and-rain statistic therefore
            pairs a local-day rainfall with a UTC-day wind figure for the same date. Documented in GSOD README.
    4. 
          
  4. Small samples. Single-date percentages come from 30 seasons; one
            season moves a date by about 3.3 percentage points. ENSO-stratified samples are smaller
            still.
    5. 
          
  5. Point-in-polygon sampling of CPC maps. Coastal cells sometimes fall
            between polygons; where that happened the nearest polygon was used and the record is
            flagged here. Cross-check against the official map image, which is shown above. Source: CPC GIS.
    6. 
          
  6. Storm Events is a reported-events database, not a complete census of
            storms. Source: NCEI Storm Events.
    7. 
          
  7. AccuWeather and other commercial providers are deliberately excluded.
            They require a paid key, their data is not open, and their output cannot be independently
            verified line by line the way a NOAA endpoint can. If you want a second opinion, you must read it directly — this project cannot vouch for it.
    8. 
        

        
What still needs work

        
    
          
  • Hourly ISD wind — done, and it changed a headline number. The
            simultaneous wind+rain statistic is now counted hour by hour from the NCEI ISD
            archive (station 72494023234) instead of pairing a whole rain day with a whole
            day’s wind. The whole-day method counts more days than can actually coincide, and
            both figures are published side by side. Remaining gap: the hourly archive’s own
            coverage is now published per season, and three of the 30 seasons it is averaged
            over are missing 1–4 of the 123 dates.
    • 
          
  • NWS forecast verification loop — scoring pipeline live, page card added. The history file, scoring code and page card (NWS verification) all exist; the first scored pairs appear once observed days accumulate past the current NWS horizon (the card says so explicitly while the count is 0).
    • 
          
  • CPC back-testing — pipeline built, archive back-fill still open. The nightly job now attempts every historical August seasprcp_YYYYMM.zip issuance (1995–2020) and samples any it retrieves with the same point-in-polygon code used for live outlooks, but CPC's live server retains only recent months, so the CPC back-test card reports pending-backfill with the attempt table until per-issuance URLs inside the official Oct-1995 archive are followed. The IRI mirror was vetted and rejected as a source. See docs/NEXT_SESSION.md §2.1.
    • 
          
  • Atmospheric river awareness — done. Storm-pattern language is scanned out of the NWS Area Forecast Discussion and quoted verbatim; no date or amount is ever attached to a quotation. Remaining gap: the scan counts language, it cannot yet say how many days a discussion covers.
    • 
          
  • Humidity for climatology days is not an observed NOAA relative-humidity
            normal. Where available, it is a labelled per-calendar-date derivation from official NCEI
            hourly temperature and dew-point normals (Magnus formula); if that source is unavailable,
            the field remains empty.
    • 
          
  • Wind record still comes from SFO (KSFO), 11.9 miles away: the closest
            long daily wind record. Hourly ISD at a nearer site would tighten it.
    • 
          
  • The wind and hourly archives themselves are not current. NCEI's
            annual GSOD and ISD files for this station end on 27 Aug 2025 while the daily
            rain/temperature record runs to this month. Every statistic here is a 1991–2020
            statistic and none of them depends on the missing months, but nothing on this page
            may be read as describing recent conditions from those archives — so the
            newest row fetched from each one, its age and its URL are published under
            Data quality.
    • 
          
  • See full list in LIMITATIONS.md
