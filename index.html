Reality check — read this first

No source on Earth forecasts a specific day in January 2027 today.

The National Weather Service publishes a daily forecast that reaches …. Beyond that, the official products are probabilities for a period (6-10 days, 8-14 days, weeks 3-4, months, 3-month seasons) — never a value for one named day.

So this scoreboard shows, for each day from 1 Oct 2026 to 31 Jan 2027, one of two things — and it always says which:

  • NWS forecast the day is inside the official forecast horizon, and every number is the actual NWS gridded forecast.
  • Climatology the day is beyond it, so every number is the 1991-2020 observed record for that calendar date at the verified NOAA station. This is not a forecast — it is what has historically happened on that date.

Long rain-duration events (“days or weeks of straight rain”)

      

        
A wet day is a calendar day with at least 0.01 in of
          liquid precipitation. A streak is consecutive wet days inside
          1 Oct–31 Jan. Counted from the actual observed record, 1991-2020, station USW00023272. For landlord: a week-long wet spell is close to a coin flip in any given year (53.3% of seasons), and a 10-day spell happens ~1 year in 4.

Sources — every single fetch

      

        
Nothing on this page is typed in by hand. Every value traces to one of the
          requests below, each recorded with its URL, HTTP status, byte count, SHA-256 and retrieval
          time. A guardrail in the build rejects any host that is not on the vetted official list (noaa.gov, weather.gov, ncei.noaa.gov, cpc.ncep.noaa.gov, census.gov). Commercial providers like AccuWeather are excluded because they require paid keys and are not independently verifiable line by line.

        

          
          
          
        

        

      

    


    
    

      
Verification — every number checked against its source

      

        
The pipeline re-reads its own datasets every night and checks, one claim at
          a time, that the number shown is the number in the official file, that the file was
          actually retrieved (with the HTTP status, byte count and SHA-256 of those exact bytes
          recorded), and that the project’s rules were respected — no day labelled a forecast
          outside the official horizon, no humidity value presented as an observation when it is a
          derivation, no NOAA test message shown as a real warning. A failed check stops the build.

        

      

    


    
    

      
Data quality — irregularities flagged for review

      

        
Anything the pipeline could not verify, or that looked wrong, is recorded
          here rather than silently smoothed over. See also VERIFICATION.md.

Structural limits of this project

        
    
          
  1. No daily forecast exists beyond the NWS horizon. Every day outside it
            shows observed climatology, clearly badged. Anyone who tells you the rainfall on
            14 January 2027 is “going to be 0.8 inches” is inventing it. Verify: forecast.weather.gov MapClick only shows 7 days.
    2. 
          
  2. Two different stations. Rain/temperature from the San Francisco
            downtown gauge USW00023272, wind from SFO 72494023234. They are ~10 miles apart, and San Francisco’s
            microclimates mean the Sunset differs from both. Sources: GHCN-Daily file and GSOD archive.
    3. 
          
  3. GSOD days are UTC days (00-24Z), i.e. roughly 16:00-16:00
            local, while GHCN rain days are local days. The joint wind-and-rain statistic therefore
            pairs a local-day rainfall with a UTC-day wind figure for the same date. Documented in GSOD README.
    4. 
          
  4. Small samples. Single-date percentages come from 30 seasons; one
            season moves a date by about 3.3 percentage points. ENSO-stratified samples are smaller
            still.
    5. 
          
  5. Point-in-polygon sampling of CPC maps. Coastal cells sometimes fall
            between polygons; where that happened the nearest polygon was used and the record is
            flagged here. Cross-check against the official map image, which is shown above. Source: CPC GIS.
    6. 
          
  6. Storm Events is a reported-events database, not a complete census of
            storms. Source: NCEI Storm Events.
    7. 
          
  7. AccuWeather and other commercial providers are deliberately excluded.
            They require a paid key, their data is not open, and their output cannot be independently
            verified line by line the way a NOAA endpoint can. If you want a second opinion, you must read it directly — this project cannot vouch for it.
    8. 
        

        
What still needs work

        
    
          
  • Hourly ISD wind to match wind days to local calendar days and detect simultaneous wind+rain hour by hour (currently day-level approximation).
    • 
          
  • CPC back-testing — score past CPC outlooks against what actually happened at 94122 to say “when CPC tips above-median for OND here, it verified X% of the time”.
    • 
          
  • Atmospheric river awareness — parse AR language from NWS Area Forecast Discussion (already fetched) to add signal for long-duration events.
    • 
          
  • NWS forecast verification — store each night’s forecast and score vs observations.
    • 
          
  • Humidity for climatology days is not an observed NOAA relative-humidity
            normal. Where available, it is a labelled per-calendar-date derivation from official NCEI
            hourly temperature and dew-point normals (Magnus formula); if that source is unavailable,
            the field remains empty.
    • 
          
  • Wind record still comes from SFO (KSFO), ~10 miles away: the closest
            long daily wind record. Hourly ISD at a nearer site would tighten it.
    • 
          
  • See full list in LIMITATIONS.md
