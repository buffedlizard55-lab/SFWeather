Reality check — read this first

No source on Earth forecasts a specific day in January 2027 today.

The National Weather Service publishes a daily forecast that reaches …. Beyond that, the official products are probabilities for a period (6–10 days, 8–14 days, weeks 3–4, months, 3-month seasons) — never a value for one named day.

So this scoreboard shows, for each day from 1 Oct 2026 to 31 Jan 2027, one of two things — and it always says which:

  • NWS forecast the day is inside the official forecast horizon, and every number is the actual NWS gridded forecast.
  • Climatology the day is beyond it, so every number is the 1991–2020 observed record for that calendar date at the verified NOAA station. This is not a forecast — it is what has historically happened on that date.

Known limitations

      

        

        
Structural limits of this project

        
    
          
  1. No daily forecast exists beyond the NWS horizon. Every day outside it
            shows observed climatology, clearly badged. Anyone who tells you the rainfall on
            14 January 2027 is “going to be 0.8 inches” is inventing it.
    2. 
          
  2. Two different stations. Rain/temperature from the San Francisco
            downtown gauge, wind from SFO. They are ~10 miles apart, and San Francisco’s
            microclimates mean the Sunset differs from both.
    3. 
          
  3. GSOD days are UTC days (00–24Z), i.e. roughly 16:00–16:00
            local, while GHCN rain days are local days. The joint wind-and-rain statistic therefore
            pairs a local-day rainfall with a UTC-day wind figure for the same date.
    4. 
          
  4. Small samples. Single-date percentages come from 30 seasons; one
            season moves a date by about 3.3 percentage points. ENSO-stratified samples are smaller
            still.
    5. 
          
  5. Point-in-polygon sampling of CPC maps. Coastal cells sometimes fall
            between polygons; where that happened the nearest polygon was used and the record is
            flagged here. Cross-check against the official map image, which is shown above.
    6. 
          
  6. Storm Events is a reported-events database, not a complete census of
            storms.
    7. 
          
  7. AccuWeather and other commercial providers are deliberately excluded.
            They require a paid key, their data is not open, and their output cannot be independently
            verified line by line the way a NOAA endpoint can.
